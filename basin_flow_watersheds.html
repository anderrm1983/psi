How the daily curves are built. For each watershed with a spreadsheet and each decade, the regional annual shape is stretched so its low point lands on the typical minimum day, then reshaped and scaled until the annual total and the low-flow value match the spreadsheet exactly. If "Include storm peak" is on, a short storm pulse is added on the typical peak day and the curve is re-fitted so the annual total still matches and the highest day equals the decade's mean annual peak. Peak and minimum days vary a lot from year to year, so "typical day" is the circular average of the ten annual dates.

† Cedar and Sammamish have no spreadsheet, so they follow the regional annual trend (2020s and 2040s shapes) with no peak or low-flow fitting. Their size is set from the five gauged watersheds: runoff per square kilometre, times the basin area.

Taper. Every stream segment carries a share of the watershed's outlet flow. With the R export that share is its drainage area divided by the outlet's (so tributaries are thin and the main stem thickens toward the mouth). The built-in traces use a straight-line taper instead. Width follows flow to the 0.45 power so small streams stay visible next to the Puyallup, on one scale across both maps.

Limits. Streams hidden behind ridges in the render are still drawn on top of them. Spikes last only a few days, so slow playback makes them easier to catch.
